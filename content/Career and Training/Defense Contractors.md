## Defense Contractors

Defense contractors play a major role in developing and supporting the Electronic Warfare capabilities used by today's military. These companies design everything from radar systems and electronic sensors to jamming equipment, communications networks, and advanced software used across all branches of the armed forces. Many veterans transition into these organizations because they already understand how military systems operate in real-world environments.

### Major Employers

Several companies are considered leaders in Electronic Warfare and electromagnetic spectrum technologies, including:

- L3Harris Technologies
- Lockheed Martin
- Northrop Grumman
- RTX (Raytheon)
- BAE Systems
- General Dynamics

These organizations hire professionals for positions such as Systems Engineer, RF Engineer, Software Engineer, Electronic Warfare Analyst, Field Service Engineer, and Technical Instructor. Many projects involve supporting military modernization programs, developing next-generation Electronic Warfare systems, or maintaining existing equipment used by the Department of Defense.

### Why Military Experience Matters

Although most engineering positions require a college degree, military Electronic Warfare experience provides practical knowledge that cannot easily be learned in a classroom. Veterans often possess experience operating communications equipment, understanding tactical decision making, troubleshooting complex electronic systems, and working within classified environments. These skills complement formal education and frequently make candidates more competitive when applying for defense industry positions.

### Continuing Professional Development

Many defense contractors encourage employees to pursue advanced education and industry certifications while working. Degrees discussed in [[Education & Certifications]] along with certifications such as Security+, CISSP, or networking certifications can significantly improve advancement opportunities. Combining military experience with continued learning creates a strong pathway into technical leadership and engineering roles throughout the defense industry.
