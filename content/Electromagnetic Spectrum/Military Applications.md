## Military Applications

Military organizations rely on the electromagnetic spectrum for nearly every aspect of modern warfare. Communications, intelligence gathering, navigation, targeting, radar systems, satellite communications, and unmanned aircraft all depend on access to specific portions of the spectrum. Because these capabilities are essential to military operations, controlling the electromagnetic spectrum has become just as important as controlling land, air, sea, space, and cyberspace.

## Battlefield Uses

Military applications include:

- Secure radio communications
- Radar detection and tracking
- Satellite communications
- GPS navigation
- Electronic surveillance
- Intelligence collection
- Unmanned aircraft control
- Precision-guided weapons

Unlike civilian users, military forces must continue operating while adversaries actively attempt to jam, intercept, or deceive these systems.

## Relationship to Electronic Warfare

Electronic Warfare exists because modern military operations depend so heavily on the electromagnetic spectrum. [[Electronic Attack]] attempts to disrupt or deny an enemy's use of the spectrum, while [[Electromagnetic Defense]] protects friendly systems from similar attacks. [[Electromagnetic Support]] identifies and analyzes signals that help commanders understand the battlefield. Together these three functions compete for control of one of the most valuable resources in modern warfare.
