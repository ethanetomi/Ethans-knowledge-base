# Electronic Warfare Fundamentals

Electronic Warfare (EW) is the military use of the electromagnetic spectrum to gain an operational advantage over an adversary. Modern military forces depend on radio communications, radar systems, satellite navigation, wireless networking, and countless other electronic systems to accomplish their missions. Electronic Warfare focuses on controlling this environment by protecting friendly systems while disrupting or exploiting those of an opponent.

## The Three Components

Electronic Warfare is traditionally divided into three primary functions:

- [[Electronic Attack]]
- [[Electromagnetic Defense]]
- [[Electromagnetic Support]]

Each function has a unique purpose, but all three operate together throughout military operations. Information collected through Electromagnetic Support may identify enemy systems, Electronic Attack may disrupt those systems, and Electromagnetic Defense ensures friendly equipment continues operating despite enemy attempts to interfere.

## Why Electronic Warfare Matters

Modern warfare extends far beyond conventional weapons. Communications, navigation, intelligence gathering, and precision targeting all rely on the electromagnetic spectrum. Because of this dependence, controlling the spectrum has become just as important as controlling land, sea, air, space, and cyberspace. Nations capable of dominating the electromagnetic environment gain a significant operational advantage before physical engagements even begin.
