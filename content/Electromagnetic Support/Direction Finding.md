# Direction Finding

Direction Finding is the process of determining the direction and possible location of a radio frequency emitter. Instead of only recognizing that a signal exists, Direction Finding attempts to answer where that signal is coming from. This makes it one of the most useful capabilities within [[Electromagnetic Support]], especially when enemy radios, radar systems, or data links need to be located.

## How Direction Finding Works

A Direction Finding system receives a signal and measures the direction from which it arrived. A single receiving site may provide a bearing toward the transmitter, but it may not provide an exact location. When multiple sites collect bearings from the same signal, the information can be compared to estimate the emitter's position through triangulation.

The basic process can be broken down into the following steps:

1. Detect the signal.
2. Analyze its frequency and characteristics.
3. Calculate the direction of arrival.
4. Compare information from multiple collection sites.
5. Report the estimated emitter location.

## Operational Uses

Once an emitter is located, the information may support intelligence collection, maneuver planning, threat avoidance, or targeting. For example, locating an enemy radar system could allow friendly aircraft to avoid the area or allow [[Electronic Attack]] assets to plan a jamming mission. Locating enemy communications may also reveal the position of a command post or other important unit.

Direction Finding does not automatically prove the identity of a transmitter. The location must still be combined with signal analysis, intelligence, and the surrounding tactical situation. When used correctly, it gives commanders a more complete understanding of where electronic activity is occurring.
