# Signal Detection and Identification

Signal Detection and Identification is the process of discovering electromagnetic activity and determining what type of system may be producing it. This is often the first step of an Electromagnetic Support mission because an operator must find and understand a signal before it can be located, monitored, or targeted.

## Detecting a Signal

Electronic systems continuously scan selected portions of the [[Electromagnetic Spectrum]] for activity. When a transmission appears, the system may measure characteristics such as:

- Frequency
- Signal strength
- Bandwidth
- Modulation
- Pulse timing
- Duration
- Direction of arrival

These characteristics act like identifying features. Different radios, radar systems, and data links may transmit in recognizable patterns that help analysts determine what type of equipment is operating.

## Identification and Analysis

After a signal is detected, it must be compared with known information. Analysts may determine whether the signal belongs to a friendly force, civilian system, unknown source, or possible adversary. Correct identification is important because the electromagnetic environment is crowded, and not every unusual signal represents a threat.

Once a signal is identified, it may be monitored over time to understand how the system is being used. Changes in transmission patterns may indicate movement, preparation for an operation, or a change in enemy activity.

## Supporting Other Missions

Signal Detection and Identification provides information that can support [[Direction Finding]], intelligence collection, and force protection. It may also help [[Electronic Attack]] personnel select the correct frequency for jamming while helping [[Electromagnetic Defense]] personnel recognize threats against friendly systems. Without proper detection and identification, other Electronic Warfare actions may be less accurate or could affect the wrong signals.
